Read · Parents, kids and sharing images

A toddler is shouting because he does not want his lunch. His parents film the moment and soon post it online. A teenager plays a new song on guitar; his mother films him, and later he discovers other people are watching the video.

Parents in the UK can post hundreds of images of their children before they are five. This practice is often called sharenting. Younger children may not care, but older children and teenagers can feel differently about old or embarrassing images.

Parents usually share with good intentions — for example, to include grandparents and extended family who live far away. The problem is that some users do not check privacy settings carefully and may share more widely by accident.

In some countries, children’s permission matters legally as well as personally. The key idea is simple: parents should listen to older children and agree what can be shared and with whom. Then family memories can remain enjoyable rather than embarrassing.

Developing writing ОГЭ: email

An informal email · Introducing yourself
From: Maya · Subject: school exchange

Hi! I’m Maya. I’m from Brighton in the UK. Let me tell you about myself.

I’ve got one brother and one sister. My sister and I are almost the same age and we go everywhere together. My father’s a teacher and my mother works in an office. What about you?

I love listening to music and I also enjoy team sports like football and basketball. What do you like doing in your free time?

My favourite subjects are art and English. This year I have special art classes on Saturdays. At the moment I’m reading a book about Frida Kahlo.

Anyway, that’s all for now. Is there anything special you’d like to know about me? Write back soon. Best wishes, Maya

Reading strategy

Evidence hunt

After the Sharenting text, do one extra ОГЭ habit: for every answer, point to the exact sentence or idea in the text that proves it. Do not choose only because the same word appears in the option.

Lexicogrammar

Form first, meaning second

Use the noun-suffix and tense exercises as ОГЭ training. Before typing, identify what the gap needs: noun / verb / adjective / tense form. Then check spelling and agreement.

Fast routine: part of speech → grammar signal → form → spelling → reread the whole sentence.
Oral Task 2

Six-question survey rehearsal

Answer these six questions aloud. Give a direct full answer and one relevant detail where natural.

  1. Who is in your immediate family?
  2. How often do you see your relatives?
  3. What do you usually do with your family at weekends?
  4. What causes arguments between parents and teenagers?
  5. What is your favourite school subject and why?
  6. What would you recommend to a teenager who wants a better relationship with their parents?
Oral Task 3

Family monologue · 10–12 sentences

You are going to give a talk about family relationships. You will have 1.5 minutes to prepare and should speak for not more than 2 minutes.

Remember to say: • who you are closest to in your family; • what families can enjoy doing together; • what can cause arguments between parents and teenagers; • what your attitude to spending time with family is.
Writing · Task 35

Personal email · 100–120 words

Use the textbook email to Maya first. Then do this exam-style transfer.

…My family and I spend a lot of time together. What do you usually do with your family at weekends? Who are you closest to in your family? What causes arguments between parents and teenagers in your opinion?…

Write a reply and answer all three questions. Write 100–120 words.
